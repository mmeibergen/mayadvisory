01 \ The line-up

Six minds, each with a job it does best

01 \

ChatGPT

The everyday generalist

Broad, fast, and forgiving. The one to reach for when you just need a capable answer without much setup.

  • \ Quick explanations and first drafts
  • \ Travel, food, and everyday planning
  • \ Reformatting and tidying up text
  • \ Images and one-off odd jobs

Tip

Your default for quick, low-stakes asks. Keep prompts plain and conversational, and it rarely lets you down.
02 \

Claude

The thought partner

Strong at nuance, structure, and holding a long argument together. Use it when the quality of the thinking matters more than the speed.

  • \ Long-form writing and editing
  • \ Research synthesis and analysis
  • \ Brainstorming and problem structuring
  • \ Reviewing your own reasoning

Tip

Hand over your raw material and ask it to push back, not just agree. The disagreement is the value.
03 \

Gemini

The live wire

Built on Google's backbone, so it reaches the open web well and grounds answers in what it finds.

  • \ Latest news and market moves
  • \ Live prices, facts, and figures
  • \ Anything tied into Search, Maps, Gmail, Docs
  • \ Source-backed summaries

Tip

Your fact-checker. When the answer could have changed this month, start here.
04 \

Grok

The contrarian

Looser on edgy subjects and plugged into live social feeds, so it surfaces takes the others tend to trim.

  • \ Controversial or charged topics
  • \ Arguments others soften
  • \ Real-time pulse of X and social
  • \ Pressure-testing a strong view

Tip

Treat it as one voice, not the verdict. No model is truly neutral, so triangulate before you trust.
05 \

Codex

The engineering agent

OpenAI's coding agent. It lives in your codebase and terminal rather than a chat box, writing, running, and debugging real software end to end.

  • \ Writing and refactoring code
  • \ Debugging and fixing failing tests
  • \ Building features across a repo
  • \ Automating dev and shell workflows

Tip

Describe the outcome, not every step. Let it run, then read the diff and review before you merge.
06 \

Perplexity

The answer engine

Built for research, not chat. It searches the live web and hands back a direct answer with every source cited, so you can follow the trail and check the claim.

  • \ Researching an unfamiliar topic
  • \ Answers with citations you can verify
  • \ Comparing options or vendors
  • \ Deep-dive reports with references

Tip

Follow the citations, not just the summary. Its real strength is showing you where each claim came from.

02 \ Tips & tricks

Habits that beat hunting for the perfect model

01 \

Triangulate what matters

Ask the same hard question to two models and read where they disagree. The gap is where the real thinking lives.

02 \

Prompt over product

Context, an example, and a clear ask outperform switching tools. Most weak answers trace back to a thin prompt.

03 \

Draft in one, edit in another

Generate in the first, paste into the second, ask it to critique. The handoff catches a surprising amount.

04 \

Verify the confident answer

Fluency is not accuracy. For numbers, names, and dates, follow the link and check the source yourself.

The 10-second cheat sheet

ChatGPT \

Quick, low-stakes, everyday. The everyday default.

Claude \

Writing, research, real thinking. The thought partner.

Gemini \

Latest, live, Google-connected. The fact-checker.

Grok \

These are tendencies, not laws. The models leapfrog each other constantly, so re-check your defaults every few months and let the task, not habit, decide.
